The Anatomy of Ransomware: How it Works and How to Defend Against It

May 9, 2026 Cybersecurity, Malware

Ransomware is a type of malicious software designed to block access to a computer system or files until a sum of money is paid. Over the past decade, it has evolved from simple screen-lockers to sophisticated encryption tools that target large organizations and critical infrastructure.

How Ransomware Operates

Most ransomware attacks begin with a phishing email containing a malicious attachment or a link. Once the user clicks, the malware is downloaded and begins executing. It typically communicates with a Command and Control (C2) server to receive an encryption key. After that, it scans the system for valuable files—documents, images, databases—and encrypts them using strong algorithms like AES or RSA.

Modern ransomware often employs "double extortion," where attackers not only encrypt the data but also exfiltrate it, threatening to release sensitive information publicly if the ransom isn't paid.

Defense Strategies

Defending against ransomware requires a multi-layered approach. Regular, offline backups are crucial—if your files are encrypted but you have a recent backup, you can simply restore the system without paying the ransom. Additionally, employing endpoint detection and response (EDR) solutions, keeping software updated to patch vulnerabilities, and conducting regular security training for employees can significantly reduce the risk of infection.

Demystifying AI: Classification vs. Regression Explained

May 1, 2026 Machine Learning, AI

When diving into supervised machine learning, you'll quickly encounter two fundamental types of problems: classification and regression. While both involve training a model on historical data to make predictions, they serve entirely different purposes based on the kind of output you need.

Classification: Predicting Categories

Classification is used when the output variable is categorical. The goal is to map input data to a specific class or category. For example, filtering emails into "Spam" or "Not Spam," diagnosing a medical image as "Benign" or "Malignant," or determining the species of a flower based on its petal dimensions.

Common classification algorithms include Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machines (SVM), and Random Forests. The model output is typically a probability that the input belongs to a certain class.

Regression: Predicting Continuous Values

Regression, on the other hand, is used when the output variable is a real, continuous value. Instead of sorting data into buckets, regression models try to predict a quantity. Examples include predicting the price of a house based on its square footage and location, forecasting next month's sales, or estimating the temperature for tomorrow.

Linear Regression, Ridge Regression, and Gradient Boosting Regressors are popular choices here. The performance is usually measured by how close the predicted values are to the actual values (e.g., Mean Squared Error).

In short, if your question is "Which one?" or "Is it X or Y?", you're looking at a classification problem. If your question is "How much?" or "How many?", you need regression.
